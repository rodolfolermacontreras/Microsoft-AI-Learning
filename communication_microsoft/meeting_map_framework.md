# EEO Meeting Map Framework

> Build a plan to get alignment for high-stakes meetings and moments.
> Source: EEO Meeting Map PDF (4 pages).

---

## What Is the Meeting Map?

The Meeting Map is an artifact you complete **before building presentation slides**. It saves time by aligning the team on critical components before significant work begins.

---

## Meeting Map Components

| Component | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| **Audience** | Identifies primary and secondary target audience |
| **Meeting Purpose** | Simple articulation of what we are solving for and the key messages that need to land |
| **Desired Outcomes** | What does success look like? What do you want the audience to think, feel, and do? |
| **Context** | Critical background information to ensure alignment |
| **Stakeholder Matrix** | Identifies key stakeholders and who owns what |
| **Workback Plan** | Maps the plan leading up to the meeting, including sequencing |

### Primary Audience Takeaways

| Dimension | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Think** | What should the audience think after this meeting? |
| **Feel** | What should the audience feel after this meeting? |
| **Do** | What should the audience do as a result of this meeting? |

### Context

Bulleted list that succinctly summarizes critical context. Consider:

- What is the business driver for this meeting (e.g., competitive threat, technology trend, business performance issue, budget cuts, new Microsoft priority)?
- Are there any critical or related initiatives or executive discussions happening that might impact this meeting or outcomes?
- How does your audience like to consume information?
- What internal or external pressures are being applied to your audience members that impact how they think about your desired outcomes?
- What is preventing your primary audience from "thinking, feeling, or doing" any of your desired outcomes at this time?

### Stakeholder Matrix

| Name | Role and Org | Disposition | Actions Needed | Required Socialization Points |
|------|-------------|-------------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| Name of individual | Reviewer / Approver / Consult / Informed | Supporter / Detractor / On-the-fence | Be specific about all the things you need from this person | All the things you need this person to weigh-in on and/or support |

**Disposition key:**
- **Supporter** -- Already aligned with your desired outcomes.
- **Detractor** -- Opposed or skeptical; needs specific attention.
- **On-the-fence** -- Undecided; may be influenced by the right framing.

**Role key (RACI-style):**
- **Reviewer** -- Reviews content or decisions.
- **Approver** -- Has final sign-off authority.
- **Consult** -- Provides input but does not decide.
- **Informed** -- Needs to know the outcome but does not participate in decisions.

## How to Use This as a Data Scientist

When preparing a data review, model readout, or insights presentation:

1. **Start with the Meeting Map** before opening PowerPoint or a notebook.
2. **Define "Think, Feel, Do"** -- this forces you to articulate the business impact, not just the technical result.
3. **Use the Stakeholder Matrix** to identify who needs pre-socialization (e.g., your skip-level, partner teams).
4. **Context section** is where you capture the "why now" -- connect your analysis to the business driver.
5. **Workback Plan** prevents last-minute scrambles -- schedule dry runs and review cycles.
